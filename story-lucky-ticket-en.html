"Lucky ticket" 💘

Author: Olga Kobylska | Translator: Helen Kapshytar | Theme: New Year, Magic & Destiny 💘

It was an ordinary winter evening outside. I was riding in a minibus and looking into a small melted circle on the glass. Outside the window, there were familiar grey houses, dark trees covered in fluffy caps of snow flashing by, and somewhere holiday shop windows flashed with multi-colored lights. New Year would come soon, in about two weeks. But for some reason there was no joy, no fairy tale in my soul. Oh, I hadn’t even started to prepare yet: what should I choose for Alenka and my mother, and would there be enough money for a Christmas tree?..

The loud bang of the bus door brought me out of my thoughts. I was lucky that I managed to take a seat today, otherwise I would have had to jostle for forty minutes among the same tired passengers. So, we'd already passed halfway. Having nothing else to do, I looked around the salon - nothing interesting - ordinary people returning home. I checked if the bag was closed, just in case. And then I began to examine the ticket, which I held tightly in my palm. I remembered how in childhood we added the first three digits separately and the last ones too.

If you got equal numbers, it meant you would have great luck, and something very, very good would happen with you. Should I try it? Here! Happened! "Lucky ticket"! Hooray! It was a small thing, but it lifted my spirits. Maybe there would really be some kind of surprise?

Alas, everything was as usual. We played with Alyonushka, discussed all the kindergarten secrets and read our favorite “Tsokotukha Fly” at night. Mom, having fed me with delicious borscht and fluffy pies, went off to watch TV. And I stayed to clean up the kitchen. “Should I buy a lottery ticket, there must be some luck?” – I thought with a smile, although I was also glad that my family was doing well.

The unexpected phone call frightened me. I had already got into bed to read a little before sleeping, and here – the call! Who was it that bothered people in the middle of the night?

- Katka! Hello! Still awake?

Well, how could you not be rude in response to such a question at half past eleven? But I had to restrain myself - after all, that cheerful voice belonged to my cousin Tanya.

- God! Tanya! How we missed you! Where have you been? How are you? Tell us!

- Katyusha, darling! Sorry it's late. Haven’t I woken Alenka up? Is Kira Petrovna already asleep?

We briefly began to tell our news. The main one was the news that Tatyana was getting married.

– Anton is simply a miracle! When you come, you'll see. Yes, that’s why I’m calling: we invite you and your baby to celebrate the New Year! Or do you have any plans? I didn’t have any ideas (except, like watching TV, quietly) for spending a festive night. But I still resisted: - We will only disturb you... - Oh, don’t make me laugh: “you” and “disturb” are not compatible! So, we are waiting for you on the 31st in the morning. Santa Claus has already booked he sleigh for Alenka. And something for you...

It was a truly fabulous New Year! So much joy, warmth and fun in three winter days! There was enough for the whole year. Santa Claus gave Alyonushka a sleigh, a hare, a bag of goodies, and even fulfilled a dream - we went to a real circus! And I simply got love and a teddy bear...

Tanya and Anton, cunning creatures, decided to introduce me to Maxim (Anton’s older brother). But neither he nor I had been told about this.

The surprise was a success. I really liked Maxim.

When a cheerful crowd of us went out into the frosty night to see the lights of the New Year tree, Maxim asked me:

-Are you in love?

“Yes, especially in people like you,” I decided to joke.

- But not me. Usually, no. And here - at first glance. Probably New Year’s magic,” he said seriously and took my hand tightly. With his other hand he held Alyonka, who was dozing on his shoulder.

A winter fairy tale spun us around in a happy round dance. But it's time to return home. “Marry me,” Maxim suggested before we left. Life had taught me not to believe in miracles.

- Let's wait until spring, when our fabulous snowflakes melt, then we'll see.

- OK we will wait.

And here again ordinary everyday life: snow gave way to slush, blizzards to wind and rain. Work, kindergarten, home... I was a fool, a fool! I was happy with him! And he was good too - no call, nothing...

- Mother! Hooray! Mum has come home!

“Before I had time to turn the key in the lock, Alenka’s joyful squeal was heard.

- Mommy, look who came to us! Uncle Maxim!

From the doorway I flew straight into my own man’s arms. And the calendar on the wall waved a leaf: “March 1st.”

Curled up in the arms of my beloved, I listened to his story:

“And can you imagine, when I was on my way to see you, I came across a “lucky ticket.” Here it is, shall we keep it? Fine?

- Fine!

It was good when there was happiness at home.
